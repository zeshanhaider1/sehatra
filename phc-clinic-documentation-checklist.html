A practical guide for Punjab clinic owners

Clinic paperwork, without the last-minute scramble

When a document is needed, can your team find the latest copy quickly? This checklist helps you organise the records you already use, spot gaps and decide what needs a closer look.

Start here: Clinics, hospitals, laboratories and other facilities may follow different PHC standards. Use the PHC's MSDS resources for your own facility category. This is an administrative organising aid, not an official inspection checklist or a guarantee of approval.

1. Know your facility and keep the key papers together

Make one clearly labelled place for the documents your team needs to find first.

  • ☐ Note the facility name, category, location, responsible contact and actual services offered.
  • ☐ Keep copies of your registration and licensing correspondence, applications, receipts and certificates where applicable.
  • ☐ Record issue and expiry dates and the person responsible for follow-up.
  • ☐ Keep the current category-specific PHC standards and reference material accessible to the relevant team.

Confirm the applicable steps and current requirements with the PHC licensing FAQs.

2. Give every staff member an organised file

When someone joins, changes role or renews a credential, update the relevant file instead of waiting for an inspection.

  • ☐ Maintain a staff list with roles and contact details appropriate for your facility.
  • ☐ Organise appointment or engagement records, role descriptions and relevant qualifications.
  • ☐ Where professional registration applies, keep the relevant registration information and check expiry dates.
  • ☐ File orientation, training and review records that your facility uses.

Keep personal staff information access-controlled. These are organising prompts; check your category's requirements for the details that apply.

3. Make daily records easy to locate

  • ☐ List the registers and forms your facility actually uses and assign an owner to each.
  • ☐ Check that entries are readable, dated where appropriate and filed in the right place.
  • ☐ Set a routine for locating and reviewing records, including restricted access for confidential information.
  • ☐ Track missing items and who will follow up; avoid putting patient information in unsecured messages or public forms.

4. Connect policies with what happens on the ground

  • ☐ Identify policies and work instructions relevant to your declared services and facility category.
  • ☐ Record the person responsible for reviewing each document and its latest review date.
  • ☐ Keep the relevant operational logs and supporting records together, such as training, equipment checks or waste-related records where applicable.
  • ☐ When a process changes, update the corresponding document and communicate it to staff.

A template is only a starting point. It needs to reflect how your own facility works.

5. Keep a simple follow-up list

  • ☐ Write down the gap, the next action and the person responsible.
  • ☐ Add a target date, then mark the action complete when evidence is available.
  • ☐ Review the list with your team regularly; keep meeting notes when your facility uses them.
  • ☐ Maintain a secure backup and know who can access it.

For hospitals, quality-improvement and committee documentation may require additional arrangements. Check the applicable PHC manual for your facility rather than assuming every clinic follows the same structure.

Frequently asked questions

Does this checklist replace the PHC MSDS?

No. This page is a practical organising guide. Check the PHC standards and reference material for your specific category and services.

Is registration the same as a regular licence?

They are different stages in the PHC process. See the PHC licensing FAQs for the current process and applicable details.

Do all clinics and hospitals need the same files?

No single list here can cover every facility. Start with the category-specific standards and adapt your records to your actual services.

Will a template pack guarantee a licence or inspection result?

No. Documents must reflect actual practice, and the relevant authority makes its own assessment.

What should I do if a document is missing?

Record what is missing, who will follow up and the next review date. Check the relevant PHC guidance before deciding what is needed for your facility.
